=== Prose ===

Learning to build a language model starts with simple pieces. First you read text. Then you turn letters into numbers. Then you train a network to predict the next letter. Over time, the model picks up spelling, spacing, and common phrases.

Good training data uses clear sentences, normal punctuation, and varied topics. Small models cannot learn everything at once. They do best when patterns repeat in consistent formats, like questions followed by answers.

A char level model sees one letter at a time. It needs many examples of common words like the, and, is, to, and you. Short lines are easier to learn than long walls of text.

When you prompt the model, give it a few words that match the training style. If you trained on dialogue, start with User: or Assistant:. The model continues in the same pattern it saw during training.

Patience matters in machine learning. Loss may drop quickly at first, then slow down. That is normal. Train longer, use more data, or make the model slightly bigger if you need better results.

Tips for training a small model:

2. Include dialogue if you want chat-like output.
3. Train until loss stops improving.
4. Save checkpoints often.
5. Test with short prompts first.

Learning to build a language model starts with simple pieces. First you read text. Then you turn letters into numbers. Then you train a network to predict the next letter. Over time, the model picks up spelling, spacing, and common phrases.

Good training data uses clear sentences, normal punctuation, and varied topics. Small models cannot learn everything at once. They do best when patterns repeat in consistent formats, like questions followed by answers.

A char level model sees one letter at a time. It needs many examples of common words like the, and, is, to, and you. Short lines are easier to learn than long walls of text.

When you prompt the model, give it a few words that match the training style. If you trained on dialogue, start with User: or Assistant:. The model continues in the same pattern it saw during training.

Patience matters in machine learning. Loss may drop quickly at first, then slow down. That is normal. Train longer, use more data, or make the model slightly bigger if you need better results.

Learning to build a language model starts with simple pieces. First you read text. Then you turn letters into numbers. Then you train a network to predict the next letter. Over time, the model picks up spelling, spacing, and common phrases.

Good training data uses clear sentences, normal punctuation, and varied topics. Small models cannot learn everything at once. They do best when patterns repeat in consistent formats, like questions followed by answers.

A char level model sees one letter at a time. It needs many examples of common words like the, and, is, to, and you. Short lines are easier to learn than long walls of text.

When you prompt the model, give it a few words that match the training style. If you trained on dialogue, start with User: or Assistant:. The model continues in the same pattern it saw during training.

Patience matters in machine learning. Loss may drop quickly at first, then slow down. That is normal. Train longer, use more data, or make the model slightly bigger if you need better results.

Learning to build a language model starts with simple pieces. First you read text. Then you turn letters into numbers. Then you train a network to predict the next letter. Over time, the model picks up spelling, spacing, and common phrases.

Good training data uses clear sentences, normal punctuation, and varied topics. Small models cannot learn everything at once. They do best when patterns repeat in consistent formats, like questions followed by answers.

A char level model sees one letter at a time. It needs many examples of common words like the, and, is, to, and you. Short lines are easier to learn than long walls of text.

When you prompt the model, give it a few words that match the training style. If you trained on dialogue, start with User: or Assistant:. The model continues in the same pattern it saw during training.

Patience matters in machine learning. Loss may drop quickly at first, then slow down. That is normal. Train longer, use more data, or make the model slightly bigger if you need better results.

=== Prose ===

Learning to build a language model starts with simple pieces. First you read text. Then you turn letters into numbers. Then you train a network to predict the next letter. Over time, the model picks up spelling, spacing, and common phrases.

Good training data uses clear sentences, normal punctuation, and varied topics. Small models cannot learn everything at once. They do best when patterns repeat in consistent formats, like questions followed by answers.

A char level model sees one letter at a time. It needs many examples of common words like the, and, is, to, and you. Short lines are easier to learn than long walls of text.

When you prompt the model, give it a few words that match the training style. If you trained on dialogue, start with User: or Assistant:. The model continues in the same pattern it saw during training.

Patience matters in machine learning. Loss may drop quickly at first, then slow down. That is normal. Train longer, use more data, or make the model slightly bigger if you need better results.

--- physics block 1 ---
Article: Newton's First Law
Newton's first law says an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant speed, unless a force acts on it. This is also called inertia.

Article: Newton's Second Law
Newton's second law links force, mass, and acceleration: F equals m times a. A bigger force causes greater acceleration. A bigger mass resists acceleration more.

Article: Newton's Third Law
For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. When you push on a wall, the wall pushes back on you with the same strength in the opposite direction.

Article: Speed and Velocity
Speed is how fast something moves. Velocity is speed with direction. A car going 60 mph north has a different velocity than one going 60 mph south.

Article: Acceleration
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. Braking, turning, and speeding up all count as acceleration. It is measured in meters per second squared.

Article: Friction
Friction is a force that opposes motion when surfaces rub together. It helps shoes grip the ground but also slows sliding objects.

Article: Energy
Energy is the capacity to do work. Kinetic energy comes from motion. Potential energy is stored, like a raised weight or stretched spring.

Article: Conservation of Energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. A rolling ball may lose speed to heat from friction, but total energy is conserved.

Article: Waves
A wave carries energy through a medium or space. Sound is a wave in air. Light is an electromagnetic wave. Waves have wavelength, frequency, and amplitude.

Article: Electric Charge
Matter contains positive and negative charges. Like charges repel; opposites attract. Static electricity builds when charges separate, such as after rubbing a balloon.

Article: Current and Voltage
Electric current is the flow of charge through a wire. Voltage is the push that drives current. Resistance limits current, as described by Ohm's law: V equals I times R.

Article: Magnetism
Magnets have north and south poles. Like poles repel; unlike poles attract. Moving charges create magnetic fields. Earth has a magnetic field from its core.

Article: Light and Color
White light contains many colors. A prism separates them by wavelength. Objects appear colored because they reflect some wavelengths and absorb others.

Article: Heat and Temperature
Temperature measures average particle motion. Heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference. Heat flows from hot to cold until balance is reached.

Article: Pressure
Pressure is force divided by area. Atmospheric pressure is the weight of air above us. Pressure increases with depth in a fluid because more fluid sits above.

Article: Buoyancy
An object in fluid feels an upward buoyant force. If buoyancy exceeds weight, the object floats. Ships float because they displace enough water to balance their weight.

Article: Relativity Basics
Einstein's special relativity shows that time and space depend on motion. Nothing with mass can reach the speed of light. E equals m c squared links mass and energy.

Article: Quantum Basics
At tiny scales, particles behave like waves and packets called quanta. Electrons in atoms occupy discrete energy levels rather than any orbit.

Article: The Atom
Atoms have a dense nucleus of protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. The number of protons defines the element. Atoms combine to form molecules.

Article: Radioactivity
Some nuclei are unstable and decay, emitting radiation. Alpha, beta, and gamma radiation differ in penetrating power. Half-life measures decay rate.

Electric current is the flow of charge through a wire. Voltage is the push that drives current. Resistance limits current, as described by Ohm's law: V equals I times R.

Article: Magnetism
Magnets have north and south poles. Like poles repel; unlike poles attract. Moving charges create magnetic fields. Earth has a magnetic field from its core.

Article: Light and Color
White light contains many colors. A prism separates them by wavelength. Objects appear colored because they reflect some wavelengths and absorb others.

Article: Heat and Temperature
Temperature measures average particle motion. Heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference. Heat flows from hot to cold until balance is reached.

Article: Pressure
Pressure is force divided by area. Atmospheric pressure is the weight of air above us. Pressure increases with depth in a fluid because more fluid sits above.

Article: Buoyancy
An object in fluid feels an upward buoyant force. If buoyancy exceeds weight, the object floats. Ships float because they displace enough water to balance their weight.

Article: Relativity Basics
Einstein's special relativity shows that time and space depend on motion. Nothing with mass can reach the speed of light. E equals m c squared links mass and energy.

Article: Quantum Basics
At tiny scales, particles behave like waves and packets called quanta. Electrons in atoms occupy discrete energy levels rather than any orbit.

Article: The Atom
Atoms have a dense nucleus of protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. The number of protons defines the element. Atoms combine to form molecules.

Article: Radioactivity
Some nuclei are unstable and decay, emitting radiation. Alpha, beta, and gamma radiation differ in penetrating power. Half-life measures decay rate.


=== Math ===

--- math block 1 ---
Article: Addition
Addition combines quantities into a total. The symbol is plus. For example, 3 plus 5 equals 8. Addition is commutative: 3 plus 5 equals 5 plus 3.

Article: Subtraction
Subtraction finds the difference between numbers. It is the inverse of addition. For example, 10 minus 4 equals 6.

Article: Multiplication
Multiplication is repeated addition. 4 times 3 means 4 added three times: 4 plus 4 plus 4 equals 12. The result is called the product.

Article: Division
Division splits a number into equal parts. 12 divided by 3 equals 4 because three groups of four make twelve. Division by zero is undefined.

Article: Fractions
A fraction shows part of a whole. The top is the numerator; the bottom is the denominator. One half means one part of two equal parts.

Article: Decimals
Decimals use a point to show parts less than one. 0.5 equals one half. Each place to the right is one tenth of the previous place.

Article: Percentages
A percent means parts per hundred. 25 percent equals 25 out of 100, or one quarter. To find 20 percent of 50, multiply 0.20 times 50 equals 10.

Article: Order of Operations
Evaluate parentheses first, then exponents, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right. Example: 2 plus 3 times 4 equals 14, not 20.

Article: Algebra
Algebra uses letters for unknown values. Solving an equation means finding the value that makes both sides equal. If x plus 3 equals 7, then x equals 4.

Article: Linear Equations
A linear equation graphs as a straight line. Form y equals m x plus b, where m is slope and b is the y-intercept. Slope measures steepness.

Article: Area
Area measures surface size. Rectangle area equals length times width. Triangle area equals one half times base times height.

Article: Volume
Volume measures three-dimensional space. Box volume equals length times width times height. Volume is measured in cubic units.

Article: Pythagorean Theorem
In a right triangle, a squared plus b squared equals c squared, where c is the hypotenuse. If legs are 3 and 4, the hypotenuse is 5.

Article: Prime Numbers
A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11. The number 1 is not prime. Every integer above 1 factors uniquely into primes.

Article: Ratios and Proportions
A ratio compares two quantities. A proportion states two ratios are equal. If 2 over 3 equals x over 6, then x equals 4.

Article: Statistics Basics
The mean is the average. The median is the middle value when sorted. The mode is the most frequent value. Range is max minus min.

Article: Probability
Probability measures likelihood from 0 to 1. Fair coin flip: heads probability is one half. Add probabilities of mutually exclusive outcomes to get total probability.

Article: Angles
A full turn is 360 degrees. A right angle is 90 degrees. Complementary angles sum to 90. Supplementary angles sum to 180.

Article: Circles
Circumference is 2 pi r. Area is pi r squared. Pi is about 3.14159. Diameter is twice the radius.

Article: Scientific Notation
Scientific notation writes large numbers compactly. 3000000 equals 3 times 10 to the 6th power. It makes very big and very small numbers easier to compare.

=== Chemistry and Biology ===

Article: The Cell
The cell is the basic unit of life. All living things are made of one or more cells. Cells have a membrane, cytoplasm, and genetic material.

Article: Photosynthesis Review
Plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make glucose and oxygen. The equation balances energy capture with chemical storage.

Article: The Periodic Table Review
Elements are organized by atomic number. Rows are periods; columns are groups with similar traits. Metals, nonmetals, and metalloids have different properties.

Article: Chemical Reactions
In a reaction, bonds break and form to make new substances. Reactants transform into products. Mass is conserved in a closed system.

Article: Acids and Bases
Acids donate hydrogen ions in water. Bases accept them. pH scale runs 0 to 14. Seven is neutral. Lower pH is more acidic; higher is more basic.

Article: Human Heart
The heart pumps blood through the body. It has four chambers. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins return blood toward it.

Article: Immune System
The immune system defends against pathogens. White blood cells attack invaders. Vaccines train the immune system to recognize threats.

Article: Ecosystems
An ecosystem includes living organisms and their environment. Energy flows from producers to consumers. Decomposers recycle nutrients.

Article: States of Matter
Matter commonly exists as solid, liquid, or gas. Heating adds energy and can cause phase changes. Plasma is ionized gas, common in stars.

Article: Molecules and Compounds
A molecule is two or more atoms bonded together. Water is H2O: two hydrogen, one oxygen. A compound has more than one element chemically combined.

=== Physics ===

--- physics block 1 ---
Article: Newton's First Law
Newton's first law says an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant speed, unless a force acts on it. This is also called inertia.

Article: Newton's Second Law
Newton's second law links force, mass, and acceleration: F equals m times a. A bigger force causes greater acceleration. A bigger mass resists acceleration more.

Article: Newton's Third Law
For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. When you push on a wall, the wall pushes back on you with the same strength in the opposite direction.

Article: Speed and Velocity
Speed is how fast something moves. Velocity is speed with direction. A car going 60 mph north has a different velocity than one going 60 mph south.

Article: Acceleration
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. Braking, turning, and speeding up all count as acceleration. It is measured in meters per second squared.

Article: Friction
Friction is a force that opposes motion when surfaces rub together. It helps shoes grip the ground but also slows sliding objects.

Article: Energy
Energy is the capacity to do work. Kinetic energy comes from motion. Potential energy is stored, like a raised weight or stretched spring.

Article: Conservation of Energy
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. A rolling ball may lose speed to heat from friction, but total energy is conserved.

Article: Waves
A wave carries energy through a medium or space. Sound is a wave in air. Light is an electromagnetic wave. Waves have wavelength, frequency, and amplitude.

Article: Electric Charge
Matter contains positive and negative charges. Like charges repel; opposites attract. Static electricity builds when charges separate, such as after rubbing a balloon.

Article: Current and Voltage
Electric current is the flow of charge through a wire. Voltage is the push that drives current. Resistance limits current, as described by Ohm's law: V equals I times R.

Article: Magnetism
Magnets have north and south poles. Like poles repel; unlike poles attract. Moving charges create magnetic fields. Earth has a magnetic field from its core.

Article: Light and Color
White light contains many colors. A prism separates them by wavelength. Objects appear colored because they reflect some wavelengths and absorb others.

Article: Heat and Temperature
Temperature measures average particle motion. Heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference. Heat flows from hot to cold until balance is reached.

Article: Pressure
Pressure is force divided by area. Atmospheric pressure is the weight of air above us. Pressure increases with depth in a fluid because more fluid sits above.

Article: Buoyancy
An object in fluid feels an upward buoyant force. If buoyancy exceeds weight, the object floats. Ships float because they displace enough water to balance their weight.

Article: Relativity Basics
Einstein's special relativity shows that time and space depend on motion. Nothing with mass can reach the speed of light. E equals m c squared links mass and energy.

Article: Quantum Basics
At tiny scales, particles behave like waves and packets called quanta. Electrons in atoms occupy discrete energy levels rather than any orbit.

Article: The Atom
Atoms have a dense nucleus of protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons. The number of protons defines the element. Atoms combine to form molecules.

Article: Radioactivity
Some nuclei are unstable and decay, emitting radiation. Alpha, beta, and gamma radiation differ in penetrating power. Half-life measures decay rate.

=== Math ===

--- math block 1 ---
Article: Addition
Addition combines quantities into a total. The symbol is plus. For example, 3 plus 5 equals 8. Addition is commutative: 3 plus 5 equals 5 plus 3.

Article: Subtraction
Subtraction finds the difference between numbers. It is the inverse of addition. For example, 10 minus 4 equals 6.

Article: Multiplication
Multiplication is repeated addition. 4 times 3 means 4 added three times: 4 plus 4 plus 4 equals 12. The result is called the product.

Article: Division
Division splits a number into equal parts. 12 divided by 3 equals 4 because three groups of four make twelve. Division by zero is undefined.

Article: Fractions
A fraction shows part of a whole. The top is the numerator; the bottom is the denominator. One half means one part of two equal parts.

Article: Decimals
Decimals use a point to show parts less than one. 0.5 equals one half. Each place to the right is one tenth of the previous place.

Article: Percentages
A percent means parts per hundred. 25 percent equals 25 out of 100, or one quarter. To find 20 percent of 50, multiply 0.20 times 50 equals 10.

Article: Order of Operations
Evaluate parentheses first, then exponents, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right. Example: 2 plus 3 times 4 equals 14, not 20.

Article: Algebra
Algebra uses letters for unknown values. Solving an equation means finding the value that makes both sides equal. If x plus 3 equals 7, then x equals 4.

Article: Linear Equations
A linear equation graphs as a straight line. Form y equals m x plus b, where m is slope and b is the y-intercept. Slope measures steepness.

Article: Area
Area measures surface size. Rectangle area equals length times width. Triangle area equals one half times base times height.

Article: Volume
Volume measures three-dimensional space. Box volume equals length times width times height. Volume is measured in cubic units.

Article: Pythagorean Theorem
In a right triangle, a squared plus b squared equals c squared, where c is the hypotenuse. If legs are 3 and 4, the hypotenuse is 5.

Article: Prime Numbers
A prime has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11. The number 1 is not prime. Every integer above 1 factors uniquely into primes.

Article: Ratios and Proportions
A ratio compares two quantities. A proportion states two ratios are equal. If 2 over 3 equals x over 6, then x equals 4.

Article: Statistics Basics
The mean is the average. The median is the middle value when sorted. The mode is the most frequent value. Range is max minus min.

Article: Probability
Probability measures likelihood from 0 to 1. Fair coin flip: heads probability is one half. Add probabilities of mutually exclusive outcomes to get total probability.

=== Chemistry and Biology ===

--- science block 1 ---
Article: The Cell
The cell is the basic unit of life. All living things are made of one or more cells. Cells have a membrane, cytoplasm, and genetic material.

Article: Photosynthesis Review
Plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make glucose and oxygen. The equation balances energy capture with chemical storage.

Article: The Periodic Table Review
Elements are organized by atomic number. Rows are periods; columns are groups with similar traits. Metals, nonmetals, and metalloids have different properties.

Article: Chemical Reactions
In a reaction, bonds break and form to make new substances. Reactants transform into products. Mass is conserved in a closed system.

Article: Acids and Bases
Acids donate hydrogen ions in water. Bases accept them. pH scale runs 0 to 14. Seven is neutral. Lower pH is more acidic; higher is more basic.

Article: Human Heart
The heart pumps blood through the body. It has four chambers. Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins return blood toward it.

Article: Immune System
The immune system defends against pathogens. White blood cells attack invaders. Vaccines train the immune system to recognize threats.

Article: Ecosystems
An ecosystem includes living organisms and their environment. Energy flows from producers to consumers. Decomposers recycle nutrients.

Article: States of Matter
Matter commonly exists as solid, liquid, or gas. Heating adds energy and can cause phase changes. Plasma is ionized gas, common in stars.

Article: Molecules and Compounds
A molecule is two or more atoms bonded together. Water is H2O: two hydrogen, one oxygen. A compound has more than one element chemically combined.
